# Phase B: storyboard

15.0 s, 30 fps, 450 frames (0–449), 9:16 (1080 × 1920). The picture board
is [`storyboard.jpg`](storyboard.jpg): one panel per beat, pulled from the
animatic, with its frame and timecode. The cue numbers below are the
soundtrack's (`audio/synth.mjs`, `CUES`).

## The idea in one line

Someone is watching cartoons on an old TV; a 2000s-style channel bumper cuts
in, its chrome cube character swallows a glowing orange ball, and the cube
turns, in one continuous move, into an IN YOUR SHOE shoebox. The lid lifts.
Light spills out. **STAY TUNED.**

What is being teased is a moment (something is coming in November), not a
product, a price or a date. The box never opens fully: what is inside stays
inside.

## Narrative test (the brief's questions)

1. **Does it initially feel like old television content?** The first 3.2 s
   are a 2000s room with a CRT playing an original cartoon: warm, hazy light;
   a soft picture with a slight colour split, grain and faint scanlines; the
   cartoon's tune heard through the set's small speaker over room tone. No
   logo, no text.
2. **Does the channel-bumper interruption feel nostalgic?** The cartoon is
   cut, not faded: one bright frame, a deep-blue swoosh wipe across the
   screen, an ident hit, the room flickering blue; then the camera is pulled
   into the screen.
3. **Does the CGI feel faithful to the references?** It follows the supplied
   ident's grammar beat for beat (a tiny cube far away, a stretch, a flat
   landing, a pop up close with a face, rubbery squash and stretch, a centred
   locked-off camera, a radial studio with a glossy floor, the logo pose), in
   the brief's palette (deep blue, chrome, orange), without copying MBC3's cube.
4. **Does the transformation look physically connected?** One object goes all
   the way through: the cube swallows the ball, its glow leaks out of the
   seam, an orange light sweep turns the chrome to black lacquer while it
   stretches into a shoebox, the monogram rises out of its lid, it turns, and a
   second sweep leaves the wordmark on its front. There is no cut and no logo
   laid over the picture.
5. **Is the IYS reveal surprising?** The film sets up the ident's normal
   ending (the logo pose, the bright sting); the sting slows to a stop like a
   tape and the character becomes something else.
6. **Does the ending create curiosity?** The lid lifts a crack, light spills
   out, and what is inside stays inside: STAY TUNED.
7. **Is the campaign left mysterious?** No event name, date, offer, price or
   product is shown or said.
8. **Is the final result good enough for an official IYS social post?** See
   the final assessment in [`05-technical-validation.md`](05-technical-validation.md).

## On-screen text, all of it

1. `STAY TUNED.` (frames 392–449)
2. The IN YOUR SHOE wordmark and monogram: official artwork, not typeset
   (`assets/brand`).

Nothing else is written or said anywhere in the film. There is no voice-over,
no subtitles, no channel logo other than IYS's own, no date, no offer.
